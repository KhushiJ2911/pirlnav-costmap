Costmap-Conditioned PIRLNav

Does conditioning behavior cloning on a ground-truth geodesic “costmap” let a policy learn from shortest-path demos where RGB cannot — and generalize better on unseen scenes?

Khushi Jaiswal · COEP Technological University · built on PIRLNav + the WayPixel costmap idea from MASt3R-Nav

Code ↗ W&B runs ↗ PIRLNav paper ↗ Jump to results ↓

TL;DR

At a matched 8M-frame budget, conditioning the policy on the costmap generalizes better than RGB on every metric when learning from shortest-path (SP) demos — Success 3.2% vs 2.1%, SPL 2.0% vs 1.1%, softSPL 0.225 vs 0.166 on the held-out 1000-episode HM3D-v2 val split.

Honest caveat. Absolute numbers are low for both arms. That’s a shared ceiling — the run is ~0.8 epoch vs the paper’s ~33 (≈62× fewer frames, 1 GPU vs 64), and the perception setup (FOV / 5 m depth / resolution) caps everyone. The costmap can’t lift that ceiling; it does measurably better beneath it. The comparison is valid because both arms ran at the identical budget.

The question

PIRLNav trains an ObjectNav policy by behavior cloning (BC) on human demonstrations. Shortest-path (SP) demos — generated by an oracle that beelines to the goal using privileged geodesic information — are a terrible teacher for an RGB policy (the paper reports 6.4% success vs 64.1% for human demos): the policy simply can’t see the privileged signal the teacher follows.

The costmap is that signal, made visible. So the prediction is an interaction effect: SP demos should stay bad for RGB but become learnable once the policy is conditioned on the costmap — a result in neither the PIRLNav nor the MASt3R-Nav paper.

Method

The costmap sensor

An online sensor computes the ground-truth geodesic distance from every navigable grid point to the nearest goal (multi-goal shortest path over the navmesh), then back-projects the agent’s depth image onto that field to produce a 2-channel egocentric costmap:

  • Channel 0 — absolute cost (cost / max_cost): carries “how far is the goal” → the STOP cue.
  • Channel 1 — per-frame contrast-stretched cost: carries “which direction is downhill” → the steering cue.

Offline precomputed fields (engineering)

Building geodesic fields online hung training on pathological HM3D navmeshes (a single find_path could spin for hours). The fix: precompute all fields offline in hard-terminate()-able subprocesses (Euclidean fallback for the rare degenerate scene), then load them from disk at train and eval time — so training can never hang on a field build.

Why the absolute numbers are low

Two entangled causes cap every arm — this is the project’s honest core finding, and it is not a costmap problem:

1 · Undertraining (budget)

We trained ~0.8 of one epoch. The paper trains BC for ~500M steps on 64 GPUs. The LR schedule decays to ~0 by 8M steps, so learning is forced to stop after less than one pass over the data.

2 · Representation ceiling

FOV / 5 m depth window / low resolution. The costmap is back-projected through the same limited depth camera, so it inherits the same ceiling — it can only reveal cost within the visible window.

Evidence this is a real ceiling: in the earlier internship reproduction, even RGB + human demos scored ~0% on val (vs the paper’s 64.1%). Demo type isn’t the bottleneck; budget and perception are.

Engineering notes

Training the costmap arm to completion on rented GPUs surfaced four distinct failure modes, each fixed:

  • NaN divergence (from-scratch encoder + sharp SP targets) → lr 5e-4 + grad-clip 0.1 + a non-finite-gradient guard.
  • Field-build hangs on pathological navmeshes → offline precompute with hard-terminate() timeouts + disk loading.
  • CUDA “unspecified launch failure” crashes (intermittent) → a self-healing wrapper that auto-resumes from the last checkpoint on any crash and kills+resumes on any hang, marching to 25k updates across restarts (final run: 4 attempts, 3 crashes, all recovered automatically).
  • Sim/CUDA contention → render on a separate GPU from training.

Limitations & future work

  • Lift the ceiling, then re-run the same comparison — wider FOV, deeper depth window, higher-resolution costmap, perhaps a spatial map. This is what would let the costmap’s advantage actually show in absolute terms.
  • Full-budget training (multiple epochs, stretched LR schedule) to test how much of the low absolute success is undertraining.
  • SP demos never teach exploration; a policy that can only follow a visible cost gradient is stuck when the goal is out of perceptual range.
